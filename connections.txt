LARKEN DESK COMPANION - WIRING REFERENCE
========================================

Target: ESP32-C3 SuperMini, ILI9341 SPI TFT for development, two TTP223 touch
modules. Firmware supports an ST7789 panel by changing LARKEN_USE_ST7789 in
Larken_VS.ino and matching the panel dimensions below.

WI-FI / BREADBOARD PLACEMENT
----------------------------
The ESP32-C3 SuperMini normally has a small PCB antenna integrated at one end
of the module. It has no external antenna socket; no separate antenna needs to
be purchased or wired. Confirm the antenna end on your exact board revision.
Do not bury that end between breadboard contacts. Keep it just beyond the
breadboard edge with about 15-20 mm of clear space around it. Keep the TFT,
its wiring, metal, batteries, and ground planes away from that area. Changing
TFT or touch GPIO assignments will not fix antenna detuning or shielding.

If Wi-Fi works with the ESP board lifted out of the breadboard, first keep the
same wiring and power source and move only the antenna end into clear space.
Then test with the TFT/backlight disconnected. A change that follows antenna
position points to RF detuning/shielding; a change that follows the TFT load,
USB cable, or breadboard power rails points to a supply/contact/short issue.
Inspect for bent pins and accidental shorts before reinserting the board.

Wi-Fi transmit bursts can expose weak breadboard or USB power connections.
Use a stable regulated supply with a common ground and short power leads. Do
not power the display backlight from an ESP GPIO, and do not connect 5 V to a
3V3 rail. The setup firmware reports AP startup status, IP, and channel to
Serial Monitor at 115200 baud; boot messages identify whether startup reached
TFT initialization, and a heartbeat is printed every 3 seconds. The setup AP
starts automatically on first boot when no home Wi-Fi credentials are saved,
and requests 2.4 GHz channel 1. It uses AP-only mode until credentials are
saved. Both sketches currently cap Wi-Fi transmit power at 8.5 dBm to test
radio current draw; this also reduces range. Restore full/default power by
removing the setTxPower call if range becomes inadequate. While AP+STA is
active, the ESP32 may follow the home router's channel.

PIN MAP USED BY THE FIRMWARE
----------------------------
ESP32-C3 GPIO 4  -> TFT SCK / SCL / CLK
ESP32-C3 GPIO 6  -> TFT MOSI / SDA / DIN
ESP32-C3 GPIO 7  -> TFT CS
ESP32-C3 GPIO 3  -> TFT D/C / A0
ESP32-C3 GPIO 10 -> TFT RESET / RST
ESP32-C3 GPIO 0  <- TTP223 T1 OUT (main interaction sensor)
ESP32-C3 GPIO 1  <- TTP223 T2 OUT (mode/setup sensor)

TFT POWER AND SIGNALS
---------------------
TFT GND   -> ESP32-C3 GND
TFT VCC   -> power input specified by the exact TFT breakout board
TFT SCK   -> GPIO 4
TFT MOSI  -> GPIO 6
TFT MISO  -> not connected (display-only SPI)
TFT CS    -> GPIO 7
TFT DC    -> GPIO 3
TFT RESET -> GPIO 10

The ESP32-C3 GPIO pins are 3.3 V only. TFT signal inputs must be 3.3 V logic;
do not connect a 5 V logic output to a GPIO. Some TFT breakouts accept 5 V at
VCC because they include a regulator, while bare panels and other breakouts
require 3.3 V. Check the markings/datasheet for the exact module before power
is applied. Never power a bare 3.3 V panel from 5 V.

BACKLIGHT
---------
Backlight wiring differs between TFT breakout boards. If the module has a
backlight pin explicitly rated for 5 V, connect it to the board's 5 V supply
only as its documentation specifies. If it is an unregulated LED/anode pin,
do not connect it directly to 5 V: use a suitable current-limiting resistor
or a transistor driver designed for that exact panel and its backlight
current. Do not drive the backlight from an ESP32 GPIO. Tie the TFT supply
ground, ESP32 ground, and backlight-supply ground together.

TOUCH MODULES (BOTH)
-------------------
TTP223 VCC -> ESP32-C3 3V3
TTP223 GND -> ESP32-C3 GND
TTP223 OUT -> the assigned GPIO (T1 GPIO 0, T2 GPIO 1)

Power both touch boards from 3V3 so OUT cannot exceed the ESP32-C3 input
voltage. These firmware pins expect an active-HIGH TTP223 output. Verify the
module's output mode/jumper setting; some boards can be configured active-low.
Keep touch wires short and away from the TFT clock line to reduce false
triggers. GPIO 0 and GPIO 1 are selected as ordinary inputs; do not use them
for other peripherals in this wiring plan.

POWER / DECOUPLING
------------------
Use a stable, regulated supply sized for the ESP32-C3 and TFT backlight. The
USB 5 V input can power the board through its USB connector; connect grounds
as above. Do not feed 5 V into the ESP32-C3 3V3 pin. Place the listed 10 uF
capacitor across the regulated supply rail near the display/board (positive
to the rail, negative stripe/lead to GND), observing its 16 V rating and
polarity. The capacitor helps with transient load but does not replace an
adequately rated supply or any backlight current limiting.

OPTIONAL TRANSISTORS / RESISTORS
-------------------------------
The BC547, 2N3906, and resistors are not needed for the firmware as written.
Do not wire them by transistor package shape alone: BC547 and 2N3906 pin
orders vary by manufacturer/package. If a backlight driver is added, first
identify the TFT backlight voltage/current and check the transistor datasheet
pinout, base/gate drive, current rating, and required resistors. A 2N3906 is
not a substitute for a current-limiting resistor.

TOUCH CONTROLS
--------------
T1 tap       -> affectionate interaction; cycles pretend snack/water/cuddle
T1 double    -> enter/exit setup Wi-Fi access point
T1 triple    -> enter/exit developer HUD
T1 hold      -> annoyed reaction
T2 tap       -> next screen (companion, clock, status, games)
T2 hold      -> enter/exit setup mode

NOTES / LIMITS
--------------
The face, hands, eating, and drinking are screen animations only. No motors,
servos, speaker, real food/water sensing, or physical feeding hardware is
connected by this design. Hunger/thirst/energy are volatile demo values and
reset at power-up. The setup access point is open (no AP password); connect
to L.A.R.K.E.N Setup and browse to http://192.168.4.1. Enter the bot name,
owner name, whole-hour UTC offset, and home Wi-Fi details. Use setup on a
trusted nearby device. Wi-Fi credentials are stored in ESP32 Preferences/NVS;
this is not encrypted application-level storage. The device keeps home Wi-Fi
enabled for NTP time and status reporting. The timezone setting supports
whole-hour offsets only.

ARDUINO IDE LIBRARIES
--------------------
Install these libraries using Arduino IDE Library Manager:
	Adafruit GFX Library
	Adafruit ILI9341
	Adafruit ST7735 and ST7789 Library

Select an ESP32-C3 board in Arduino IDE. The sketch uses the ESP32 Arduino
core's WiFi, WebServer, and Preferences libraries. Open the root
Larken_VS.ino sketch; keep WifiEngine.h/.cpp and BotEngine.h/.cpp beside it in
the same Larken_VS folder. Arduino IDE compiles those .cpp tabs automatically.
For an ST7789 panel, set LARKEN_USE_ST7789 to 1 and set TFT_NATIVE_WIDTH /
TFT_NATIVE_HEIGHT to the panel's actual dimensions (common panels are 240x240
or 240x320). Check the panel orientation and wiring before powering it.

GRAPHICS BUFFER
---------------
BotEngine owns all display drawing. It allocates a small RGB565 face buffer
(up to 256x120 pixels, with smaller fallbacks if heap is limited), draws each
animation frame in RAM, and transfers only that face region to the TFT. Other
screens clear the display only when changing modes and update only their
changing fields afterward. A full-screen RGB565 buffer is intentionally not
used because it would consume about 150 KB on a 320x240 panel. The serial log
reports the actual face-buffer size and the measured render rate; 30 fps must
still be confirmed on the assembled hardware.

SERIAL MONITOR ON ESP32-C3
--------------------------
Select the correct ESP32-C3 board and its current COM port. Set Serial Monitor
to 115200 baud. For boards using the chip's native USB connector, enable
Tools > USB CDC On Boot > Enabled, upload, then reopen Serial Monitor on the
port that appears after upload/reset. Some SuperMini revisions use a USB-to-
UART bridge instead; select that board's serial port and leave USB CDC disabled
if that is how the board enumerates. The display selection does not affect
serial output.

ISOLATED RADIO / SERIAL TEST
----------------------------
Open wifi_radio_test/wifi_radio_test.ino as a separate Arduino sketch, select
the ESP32-C3 board and its upload port, then upload it. Set the Serial Monitor
to 115200 baud and scan near the board for ESP32-C3-SuperMini-AP. The test
password is yourpassword123. This test does not initialize the TFT, touch
sensors, saved settings, or setup portal; it starts the access point at
8.5 dBm transmit power.

Interpret the result:
	Serial text and test network visible: the USB serial and radio work; retry
		Larken_VS.ino, which now starts its setup AP on first boot if no Wi-Fi
		credentials have been saved.
	Serial text says AP=started but no network is visible: test close to the
		board with the antenna end clear, then remove TFT/backlight wiring; suspect
		antenna damage, board placement, or power integrity if still absent.
	Serial text says AP start=FAILED: record the full line; Wi-Fi did not start.
	No serial text: confirm upload reports success, then reopen the port that
		appears after reset and verify USB CDC On Boot / USB-to-UART settings above.
		The test also emits a heartbeat every two seconds if it is running.
